## Authored MCQ

Imaging shows an arterial channel from the descending aorta just beyond the left subclavian origin to the pulmonary trunk near its left branch. Which communication fits?

A. A coronary-to-right atrial fistula
B. Aortopulmonary window between ascending aorta and pulmonary trunk
C. Patent ductus arteriosus
D. An isolated ventricular septal defect

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Patent ductus arteriosus

The normal ductal insertion is near the left pulmonary artery, with its aortic end distal to the left subclavian artery.

## Authored MCQ

A moderately restrictive arterial duct carries continuous flow from aorta to pulmonary artery. Which loading mechanism is expected?

A. No chamber can be affected because the shunt is outside the heart
B. The right atrium alone receives all excess volume directly
C. Left ventricular preload must decrease in every case
D. Increased pulmonary venous return imposes left ventricular volume load

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Increased pulmonary venous return imposes left ventricular volume load

Pulmonary recirculation returns to the left atrium and ventricle, increasing left-heart volume load.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A murmur rises in late systole, continues without interruption through the second sound, then declines during diastole. Which pattern is most typical?

A. Classic continuous ductal murmur
B. Separate ventricular systolic and aortic early-diastolic murmurs
C. A pulmonary ejection murmur ending before the second sound
D. An isolated mid-diastolic mitral flow rumble

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Classic continuous ductal murmur

The classic ductal sound envelops the second heart sound with continuous systolic-to-diastolic flow.

## Authored MCQ

An adult has clubbed cyanotic toes but normal right-hand digits. Which pathway best explains the distribution?

A. A ventricular right-to-left shunt mixing uniformly before the arch branches
B. An aortopulmonary window delivering desaturated blood into the ascending aorta
C. An isolated left-to-right atrial shunt
D. Pulmonary arterial blood entering the descending aorta through a ductus distal to the right-arm supply

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Pulmonary arterial blood entering the descending aorta through a ductus distal to the right-arm supply

The ductal entry usually delivers desaturated blood to the lower body after the proximal arch vessels have branched.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

Severe pulmonary vascular disease reverses flow through a communication between the pulmonary trunk and ascending aorta. Why may both hands and feet be cyanotic?

A. The absence of differential cyanosis excludes pulmonary vascular disease
B. All reversed arterial shunts preferentially spare the hands
C. Desaturated blood enters proximal to systemic arch distribution
D. The communication is necessarily distal to the left subclavian artery

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Desaturated blood enters proximal to systemic arch distribution

A window delivers blood into the ascending aorta, unlike the usual distal ductal insertion.
