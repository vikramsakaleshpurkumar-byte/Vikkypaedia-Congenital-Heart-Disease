## Authored MCQ

A well school-age child has a vibratory midsystolic sound, loudest between the apex and lower left sternal edge when supine, with otherwise normal findings. Which explanation best fits?

A. A venous hum
B. A purely diastolic regurgitant murmur
C. A Still murmur
D. A continuous ductal murmur

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

A Still murmur

The typical Still murmur is vibratory or buzzing, midsystolic and most prominent between the apex and lower left sternal edge in the supine child.

## Authored MCQ

A neonatal systolic murmur is audible over both anterior chest regions, the axillae and the back. Which source-described mechanism is particularly relevant?

A. A cervical venous hum as the only possible explanation
B. An isolated vibration confined to the lower left sternal edge
C. Proof that fixed branch pulmonary stenosis has been excluded
D. Transient disparity between the pulmonary trunk and its branch sizes

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Transient disparity between the pulmonary trunk and its branch sizes

The neonatal pulmonary trunk–branch size disparity and branch geometry can produce widely transmitted systolic turbulence; the murmur alone does not distinguish transient physiology from fixed branch stenosis.

PDF p. 21
</details>

## Authored MCQ

A child has a continuous supraclavicular sound that is present upright and disappears with recumbency. Which diagnosis best explains this behavior?

A. A fixed valvar aortic ejection murmur
B. A Still murmur
C. A purely diastolic aortic regurgitant murmur
D. A cervical venous hum

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

A cervical venous hum

Recumbency, neutral neck position and reduced jugular flow can diminish or abolish the venous hum.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A systolic murmur is maximal above the clavicle and diminishes when the shoulders are smoothly hyperextended. Which source-described origin best fits?

A. An obligatory diastolic ductal sound
B. A continuous mammary souffle
C. Mitral regurgitation as established by location alone
D. Flow vibrations near the origins of the major brachiocephalic arteries

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Flow vibrations near the origins of the major brachiocephalic arteries

The supraclavicular arterial murmur is systolic, maximal above the clavicles and may diminish or disappear with shoulder hyperextension.

PDF p. 22
</details>

## Authored MCQ

A reviewer argues that ventricular bands must explain all Still murmurs because the two are often associated. Which observation limits that conclusion?

A. Band prevalence persists across ages while Still murmur prevalence falls toward adolescence
B. A ventricular band by itself proves an obstructive valve lesion
C. Still murmurs increase uniformly with age into adulthood
D. The source demonstrates that every ventricular band generates a murmur

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Band prevalence persists across ages while Still murmur prevalence falls toward adolescence

The mismatch between age-related murmur prevalence and band prevalence, and the source’s explicit uncertainty, prevent treating the association as a settled universal mechanism.

PDF p. 20
</details>
