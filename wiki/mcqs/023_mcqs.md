## Authored MCQ

An infant with a single main ventricle has tachypnea, poor growth and only mild cyanosis. Which hemodynamic pattern best fits?

A. Profoundly curtailed pulmonary flow as the only abnormality
B. Complete absence of any pulmonary venous return
C. A normal pulmonary circulation that cannot affect ventricular load
D. Excess pulmonary flow with ventricular volume overload

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Excess pulmonary flow with ventricular volume overload

Excess pulmonary flow produces heart failure and poor growth despite relatively mild cyanosis.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

The aorta arises from an outlet chamber connected to a single left ventricle through a progressively restrictive foramen. What consequence is described?

A. Isolated obstruction of caval return without an outflow effect
B. Subaortic obstruction with diversion toward the pulmonary circuit and greater volume burden
C. Guaranteed improvement of systemic output
D. A direct increase in the size of the systemic outlet

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Subaortic obstruction with diversion toward the pulmonary circuit and greater volume burden

Restriction creates subaortic stenosis and may augment already excessive pulmonary flow and ventricular volume overload.

## Authored MCQ

A cyanotic patient’s systolic pulmonary-flow murmur becomes shorter and softer while pulmonary blood flow falls. What explanation fits?

A. The pulmonary trunk must have moved anteriorly
B. Less blood crosses the increasingly obstructed pulmonary outlet
C. Murmur intensity directly measures oxygen saturation
D. The quieter murmur proves the obstruction has resolved

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Less blood crosses the increasingly obstructed pulmonary outlet

As stenosis increases, more flow is diverted to the systemic outlet and less traverses the pulmonary obstruction, reducing its murmur.
