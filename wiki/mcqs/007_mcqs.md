## Authored MCQ

A young patient with aortic outflow obstruction has a distinct ejection sound after S1. What does this finding most directly support?

A. Fixed subaortic obstruction as the sound source
B. A mobile stenotic aortic valve
C. Supravalvular obstruction as the sound source
D. A reliable numerical measure of stenosis severity

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

A mobile stenotic aortic valve

The sound arises from abrupt movement of a mobile doming valve and identifies valvar level rather than degree.

## Authored MCQ

A patient with known aortic stenosis develops ventricular failure, and the systolic murmur becomes softer. Which interpretation is appropriate?

A. Reduced murmur intensity does not establish reduced obstruction
B. The stenosis must have anatomically resolved
C. The sound has necessarily become a functional murmur
D. A soft murmur rules out important left ventricular outflow obstruction

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Reduced murmur intensity does not establish reduced obstruction

Aortic stenosis murmur intensity can decrease in left ventricular failure; loudness is not a stand-alone measure of obstruction.

## Authored MCQ

A closed aortic valve appears to have three segments because of a prominent raphe. Which observation is most helpful?

A. Use ascending aortic diameter alone to count valve cusps
B. Infer the leaflet count solely from murmur loudness
C. Image the open valve for two unequal doming leaflets and an elliptical orifice
D. Diagnose three functional leaflets from the closed raphe alone

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Image the open valve for two unequal doming leaflets and an elliptical orifice

Open-valve imaging reveals the bicuspid anatomy that can be obscured by a false raphe during closure.

## Authored MCQ

A stable young adult with substantial chronic aortic regurgitation has conspicuous neck pulsations. Which arterial pattern fits?

A. A normal narrow pulse pressure as the defining chronic severe pattern
B. Slow rise with a small pulse pressure and sustained peak
C. Rapid rise, wide pulse pressure and brisk collapse
D. A uniformly unimpressive upstroke with no collapse as the characteristic chronic pattern

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Rapid rise, wide pulse pressure and brisk collapse

Chronic severe regurgitation produces a rapid upstroke, wide pulse pressure, sometimes a bisferiens peak, and brisk collapse.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A patient with sudden heart failure has acute severe aortic regurgitation, but its diastolic murmur is short and unexpectedly soft. Which explanation is supported?

A. Relatively low regurgitant flow velocity can make severe acute disease acoustically unimpressive
B. A short soft murmur establishes only mild regurgitation
C. Acute and chronic severe regurgitation must have identical peripheral signs
D. Presystolic accentuation is obligatory in acute severe regurgitation

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Relatively low regurgitant flow velocity can make severe acute disease acoustically unimpressive

The source describes short, medium-pitched, disarmingly soft murmurs in acute severe regurgitation; chronic peripheral signs may be absent.
