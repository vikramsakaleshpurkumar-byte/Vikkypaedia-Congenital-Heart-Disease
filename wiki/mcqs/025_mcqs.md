## Authored MCQ

Imaging shows one great arterial trunk leaving the heart through one semilunar valve and supplying coronary, pulmonary and systemic vessels. Which anatomic description fits?

A. An isolated aortopulmonary window with two separate valves
B. Pulmonary atresia with VSD labeled as a true common trunk
C. Truncus arteriosus
D. Complete transposition with two separate arterial outlets

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Truncus arteriosus

A single great artery and single semilunar valve supply the three circulations.

## Authored MCQ

A common arterial trunk gives rise to a short main pulmonary artery that then divides into right and left branches. Which pattern is described?

A. Separate right and left ostia without a main pulmonary artery
B. Pulmonary atresia supplied only through systemic collaterals
C. Type 1 truncus
D. An isolated anomalous pulmonary vein

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Type 1 truncus

Type 1 has a short main pulmonary artery from the trunk; separate branch ostia constitute the other grouped pattern.

## Authored MCQ

During the first weeks, an infant with a common trunk becomes less cyanotic but increasingly tachypneic with poor feeding. Which change explains both findings?

A. Pulmonary blood flow has ceased completely
B. Falling pulmonary resistance increases lung flow and ventricular volume load
C. A lower pulmonary flow automatically increases left ventricular volume load
D. The common trunk has spontaneously divided into normal arteries

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Falling pulmonary resistance increases lung flow and ventricular volume load

More pulmonary flow improves systemic oxygenation but imposes volume overload and may cause severe heart failure.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

An infant has a common trunk overriding a nonrestrictive ventricular communication and significant truncal regurgitation. Which consequence is expected?

A. A nonrestrictive ventricular communication protects completely from valve dysfunction
B. Regurgitation necessarily unloads both ventricles
C. Regurgitant flow is received by both ventricles and adds to their burden
D. Only the right atrium can receive the regurgitant arterial volume

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Regurgitant flow is received by both ventricles and adds to their burden

The biventricular truncus permits regurgitant volume to load both ventricles, adding to existing hemodynamic stress.

</details>
