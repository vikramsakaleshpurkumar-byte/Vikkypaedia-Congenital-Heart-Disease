## Authored MCQ

The right atrium connects to a morphologic right ventricle that ejects into the aorta; the left atrium connects to a morphologic left ventricle that ejects into the pulmonary artery. What circulation results?

A. Normal concordance at both junctions
B. AV concordance with ventriculoarterial discordance and parallel circuits
C. A common arterial trunk supplying all three arterial beds
D. Double discordance producing a circulation in series

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

AV concordance with ventriculoarterial discordance and parallel circuits

Complete transposition combines AV concordance and ventriculoarterial discordance, creating parallel systemic and pulmonary recirculation.

## Authored MCQ

A newborn’s lung blood flow increases, but a restrictive interatrial communication prevents effective exchange between parallel circuits. Why can severe cyanosis persist?

A. Pulmonary venous blood is automatically delivered to the aorta
B. Atrial communication affects venous pressure but never oxygen exchange
C. Oxygenated blood recirculates through the pulmonary circuit instead of adequately entering systemic flow
D. Pulmonary flow alone guarantees systemic oxygen delivery

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Oxygenated blood recirculates through the pulmonary circuit instead of adequately entering systemic flow

An adequate communication permits exchange between the circuits; a restrictive atrial route can leave oxygenated blood trapped in pulmonary recirculation.
