## Authored MCQ

Imaging shows a persistent left superior vena cava entering a dilated coronary sinus and then the right atrium. No other anomaly is present. Which interpretation fits?

A. It is equivalent to direct drainage into the left atrium
B. It necessarily bypasses the lungs and causes cyanosis
C. It necessarily creates a large right-to-left shunt
D. The route is usually physiologically harmless despite coronary sinus dilation

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

The route is usually physiologically harmless despite coronary sinus dilation

Blood still reaches the right atrium through the coronary sinus, so an isolated connection need not create a physiologic derangement.

## Authored MCQ

Bilateral superior vena cavae communicate through a wide innominate bridge. The left SVC connects to the left atrium and the right SVC to the right atrium. Which flow pattern can explain absent cyanosis?

A. All left SVC blood must enter the left atrium regardless of the bridge
B. The innominate bridge closes both superior vena cavae
C. The pulmonary veins must all connect to the right atrium
D. Left atrium to left SVC, across the bridge and into the right SVC

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Left atrium to left SVC, across the bridge and into the right SVC

A freely communicating bridge can permit left-to-right flow from left atrium through the venous pathway to right atrium, reversing the expected cyanotic pattern.

## Authored MCQ

A large venous channel ascends in the posterior mediastinum to the right of the aorta and joins the right SVC after interruption of the IVC. Which description fits?

A. An anomalous descending aorta replacing systemic veins
B. Obligatory direct IVC drainage into the left atrium
C. Azygos continuation, which can be physiologically insignificant in isolation
D. Complete absence of any route for lower-body venous return

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Azygos continuation, which can be physiologically insignificant in isolation

The interrupted inferior caval flow continues through a dilated azygos system to the right SVC; isolated cases need not cause a shunt.

## Authored MCQ

A parasternal long-axis echo shows a markedly dilated coronary sinus without a known cause. Which connection should be sought?

A. Persistent left superior vena cava
B. An isolated pulmonary valve stenosis
C. Aortic coarctation as the required explanation
D. An isolated small ventricular septal defect

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Persistent left superior vena cava

An unexpectedly enlarged coronary sinus can be the clue to an unsuspected left SVC; color and contrast studies define the route.

A patient with longstanding cyanosis has normal heart size and no ECG evidence of RV hypertrophy. Imaging identifies a caval connection to the left atrium. What mechanism fits?

A. Right-heart flow is normal or reduced because some systemic venous blood bypasses it
B. The cyanosis proves increased pulmonary vascular resistance
C. The normal heart size excludes a venous shunt
D. Every right-to-left shunt must cause severe RV pressure overload

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Right-heart flow is normal or reduced because some systemic venous blood bypasses it

Anomalous caval return bypasses the right heart, unlike lesions with major RV pressure or volume overload.
