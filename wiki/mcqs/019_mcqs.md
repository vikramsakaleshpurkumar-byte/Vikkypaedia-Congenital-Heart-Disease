## Authored MCQ

Both coronary arteries arise from their appropriate aortic sinuses, but a branch drains directly into a cardiac chamber. Which lesion fits?

A. A ruptured aortic sinus aneurysm as the only possible explanation
B. An isolated aortopulmonary window
C. Anomalous left coronary artery originating from the pulmonary trunk
D. Congenital coronary arterial fistula

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Congenital coronary arterial fistula

A fistulous branch provides an abnormal drainage route despite usually appropriate coronary origins.

## Authored MCQ

A large coronary fistula empties into the right atrium. Which hemodynamic consequence is expected?

A. No chamber loading because the coronary origins are normal
B. Only isolated left ventricular loading with no passage through the right heart
C. An obligatory right-to-left shunt causing cyanosis
D. Left-to-right recirculation with right and left ventricular volume loading

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Left-to-right recirculation with right and left ventricular volume loading

Blood enters the right atrium and traverses the right heart, lungs and left heart before returning to the aorta.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

An adult has exertional ischemic symptoms and a coronary fistula with only modest measured left-to-right flow. Which mechanism remains plausible?

A. Low-resistance runoff can divert coronary blood away from myocardium
B. Only systemic desaturation can cause ischemia in this anatomy
C. A modest shunt excludes any perfusion effect
D. A fistula invariably improves distal myocardial perfusion

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Low-resistance runoff can divert coronary blood away from myocardium

Myocardial steal depends on the low-resistance alternative pathway and the coronary territory involved, not only total pulmonary shunt size.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A continuous murmur is maximal low along the sternum rather than at the usual ductal area. Which anatomic information is most useful for localizing a coronary fistula?

A. Only the patient’s systemic blood pressure
B. Only the size of the left atrial appendage
C. Only whether its coronary origin is right or left
D. The chamber or vessel receiving the fistula

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

The chamber or vessel receiving the fistula

The drainage site determines the intracardiac sound source and corresponding thoracic location.

## Authored MCQ

A patient has a diastolic murmur and angiography shows coronary runoff directly into the left ventricle. Which explanation fits the timing?

A. Every coronary fistula is necessarily audible continuously with identical configuration
B. The lesion must instead be an atrial septal defect
C. Aortic-to-ventricular pressure difference favors diastolic fistulous flow
D. The murmur proves semilunar leaflet incompetence despite the demonstrated channel

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Aortic-to-ventricular pressure difference favors diastolic fistulous flow

A coronary-to-left ventricular communication can produce an early diastolic murmur; systolic flow depends on the stoma and compression pattern.
