## Authored MCQ

A heart has an outlet ventricular communication, overriding aorta and infundibular obstruction. Which developmental displacement unifies these findings?

A. Isolated posterior displacement of the atrial septum
B. Failure of ductal closure as the primary lesion
C. Anterior and cephalad deviation of the infundibular septum
D. Isolated mitral commissural fusion

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Anterior and cephalad deviation of the infundibular septum

Outlet septal malalignment creates the ventricular defect, narrows right ventricular outflow and contributes to aortic override.

## Authored MCQ

Right ventricular outflow resistance becomes greater than systemic resistance in a child with a large malaligned ventricular communication. Which consequence follows?

A. Pulmonary flow must rise because the obstruction is tighter
B. A restrictive atrial defect becomes the only ventricular exit
C. More right-to-left flow into the aorta and less pulmonary flow
D. Aortic override alone determines flow independently of resistance

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

More right-to-left flow into the aorta and less pulmonary flow

Relative outflow and systemic resistances determine the balance; severe outflow obstruction diverts blood toward the aorta.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

An infant develops deepening cyanosis and hyperpnea, while a previously prominent systolic murmur becomes faint. Which mechanism is most consistent?

A. Increased flow across the stenosis generating less turbulence by definition
B. Closure of the ventricular defect as the usual acute event
C. A sharp decline in forward pulmonary blood flow
D. Sudden relief of all right ventricular obstruction

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

A sharp decline in forward pulmonary blood flow

Less blood traverses the stenotic pulmonary outflow, so the murmur shortens and softens despite worsening hypoxemia.

## Authored MCQ

A school-age child with exertional cyanosis habitually squats for relief. Which physiologic change contributes?

A. Immediate enlargement of the pulmonary valve annulus
B. A decrease in systemic resistance that necessarily reduces right-to-left flow
C. Increased systemic resistance and reduced return of desaturated leg blood
D. Permanent closure of the ventricular communication

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Increased systemic resistance and reduced return of desaturated leg blood

Squatting can redirect right ventricular flow toward the lungs and curtail desaturated lower-extremity venous return.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A patient has severe pulmonary obstruction but a freely nonrestrictive ventricular communication. Why is right ventricular systolic pressure usually not suprasystemic?

A. The ventricle can decompress through the communication into the systemic outlet
B. The right ventricle has no systolic contraction
C. Severe pulmonary stenosis cannot raise ventricular pressure
D. All pressure measurements must be erroneous if equal

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

The ventricle can decompress through the communication into the systemic outlet

The nonrestrictive defect imposes a systemic pressure ceiling; partial occlusion or true restriction can remove that protection.
