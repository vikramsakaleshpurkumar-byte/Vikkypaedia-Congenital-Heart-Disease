## Authored MCQ

Imaging shows no connection between the morphologic right atrium and right ventricle. Which route is essential for systemic venous return to reach the pumping chamber?

A. Direct flow through the absent right AV connection
B. Left-to-right atrial flow as the only venous exit
C. Direct entry of both caval veins into the aorta
D. Right-to-left flow across an interatrial communication

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Right-to-left flow across an interatrial communication

Systemic venous blood must cross from right atrium to left atrium and pass through the patent left AV valve.

## Authored MCQ

An infant has no right AV connection, normally related great arteries and pulmonary flow through a restrictive ventricular communication. Cyanosis deepens as that communication narrows. What mechanism fits?

A. Loss of the only systemic arterial outlet
B. A primary increase in interatrial left-to-right flow
C. Increasing subpulmonary obstruction limits lung flow
D. Relief of all pulmonary obstruction by defect closure

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Increasing subpulmonary obstruction limits lung flow

With normally related arteries, the ventricular communication provides access to the pulmonary circuit; narrowing can produce acquired pulmonary atresia.

## Authored MCQ

The pulmonary trunk arises from the main left ventricle and the aorta from the smaller right ventricle. What can narrowing of the connecting ventricular defect produce?

A. Subaortic obstruction to systemic output
B. An obligatory change to normal arterial connections
C. Isolated narrowing of pulmonary venous return
D. Improved systemic access through the small chamber

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Subaortic obstruction to systemic output

In this arrangement left ventricular blood must traverse the defect to reach the transposed aorta; restriction creates subaortic stenosis.

A cyanotic infant has a dominant left ventricular impulse and no evident right ventricular impulse. Which ECG pattern supports a lesion with a hypoplastic right ventricle and obligatory atrial shunting?

A. Left-axis deviation with adult-like precordial progression and right atrial P waves
B. A normal left atrial pattern that proves absence of an atrial communication
C. Isolated right ventricular hypertrophy with an entirely rightward axis
D. Complete AV dissociation as the defining feature

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Left-axis deviation with adult-like precordial progression and right atrial P waves

The source summary describes left-axis deviation, counterclockwise depolarization, adult precordial progression and right atrial P waves in this common arrangement.
