## Authored MCQ

An aneurysm arising from the noncoronary sinus perforates an adjacent chamber. Which receiving site is most characteristic?

A. Left ventricular apex
B. Right atrium
C. Left atrium
D. Pulmonary vein

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Right atrium

The noncoronary sinus is anatomically related to the right atrium; right coronary sinus aneurysms commonly enter the right ventricle or sometimes the right atrium.

## Authored MCQ

A previously well young adult develops a large aorta-to-right atrial communication abruptly. What makes the transition poorly tolerated?

A. A large rupture invariably reduces pulmonary flow to zero
B. All four chambers are permanently protected from recirculation
C. The heart has little time to adapt to the acute shunt volume burden
D. The systemic-to-right atrial pressure gradient must disappear immediately

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

The heart has little time to adapt to the acute shunt volume burden

Size, rapidity and receiving chamber determine the burden; sudden substantial recirculation can overwhelm adaptation.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A young adult with a long-standing systolic murmur suddenly develops chest discomfort, dyspnea and a new continuous murmur. Which complication deserves focused evaluation?

A. Isolated uncomplicated pulmonary stenosis as the explanation for continuous flow
B. Spontaneous closure as the sufficient explanation for acute deterioration
C. A new innocent flow murmur requiring no imaging
D. Rupture of a coexisting sinus of Valsalva aneurysm

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Rupture of a coexisting sinus of Valsalva aneurysm

The source highlights this change as a clue to an aortic sinus aneurysm rupturing into the right side of the heart.

## Authored MCQ

A new continuous murmur has variable systolic or diastolic accentuation and does not peak around the second sound. Which interpretation is supported?

A. Only an isolated mitral diastolic rumble can produce this pattern
B. A continuous sound excludes an aortic origin
C. This timing proves a classic uncomplicated ductus
D. A ruptured aortic sinus communication is plausible

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

A ruptured aortic sinus communication is plausible

Sinus rupture murmurs can be continuous without the characteristic second-sound peak of the classic ductal murmur.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

Imaging shows an aneurysm extending into the base of the ventricular septum in a patient with new conduction disease. Which mechanism fits?

A. A necessary change in sinus-node location
B. Injury to the AV node or His bundle near the septal base
C. Only increased flow across an anatomically normal mitral valve
D. A pure atrial-level shunt with no relation to conduction tissue

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Injury to the AV node or His bundle near the septal base

Ruptured or unruptured aneurysms penetrating the septal base can disrupt nearby conduction tissue.

</details>
