Imaging shows three thickened, poorly mobile pulmonary leaflets without commissural fusion. Which morphology fits?

A. Isolated narrowing of an intrapulmonary arterial branch
B. Typical thin mobile dome-shaped pulmonary stenosis
C. Dysplastic pulmonary valve stenosis
D. Obstruction by muscle bundles within the ventricular cavity

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Dysplastic pulmonary valve stenosis

Dysplastic pulmonary stenosis has thickened separate poorly mobile leaflets without commissural fusion.

## Authored MCQ

A high-pitched ejection sound is loudest in the second left interspace and selectively diminishes during inspiration. Which mechanism fits?

A. A fixed subvalvular diaphragm producing the valvar sound
B. An obligatory inspiratory increase identical to the tricuspid regurgitant murmur
C. Fixed immobility of dysplastic pulmonary leaflets generating a louder click
D. Reduced additional cephalad movement of a mobile pulmonary valve during ventricular contraction

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Reduced additional cephalad movement of a mobile pulmonary valve during ventricular contraction

Inspiration prepositions the mobile valve cephalad through increased right atrial contraction, reducing further excursion and the ejection sound.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

Pulmonary artery branches are stenotic and central pulmonary systolic pressure is high, but P2 has normal intensity. What explains this?

A. A high systolic pressure necessarily produces equally high diastolic pressure
B. The central pulmonary diastolic pressure can remain normal or nearly normal at valve closure
C. Every right-sided obstruction causes identical second-sound changes
D. The pulmonary valve must be absent

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

The central pulmonary diastolic pressure can remain normal or nearly normal at valve closure

Branch stenosis can raise central systolic pressure while pressure falls to near-normal diastolic levels, so valve closure need not be accentuated.

PDF p. 164
</details>

## Authored MCQ

An otherwise well adult has pulmonary trunk dilation and isolated congenital pulmonary regurgitation. Which diastolic sound pattern fits?

A. A mandatory long high-frequency murmur beginning with an accentuated P2
B. Short delayed low-to-medium-frequency crescendo-decrescendo murmur at the upper left sternal border
C. A purely holosystolic murmur confined to the lower sternal edge
D. A continuous murmur through all of systole and diastole as the defining pattern

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Short delayed low-to-medium-frequency crescendo-decrescendo murmur at the upper left sternal border

The source describes a distinctive short delayed low-to-medium-frequency diastolic murmur, sometimes louder with inspiration.

</details>
