## Authored MCQ

Echocardiography shows chordae from both mitral leaflets converging on one eccentric papillary muscle. Which morphology fits?

A. Parachute mitral valve
B. Supravalvular ring defined by an atrial diaphragm at the leaflet bases
C. An intra-atrial membrane proximal to the appendage
D. Double-orifice valve defined by two separate annular openings

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Parachute mitral valve

A single papillary muscle receiving all chordae defines the parachute arrangement and can restrict interchordal flow.

## Authored MCQ

A parachute mitral valve prompts a search for associated left-sided obstruction. Which combination is the classic developmental complex?

A. Supravalvular mitral ring, parachute mitral valve, subaortic stenosis and coarctation
B. Parachute mitral valve, pulmonary valve stenosis, atrial septal defect and ductus
C. Cor triatriatum, pulmonary atresia, aortic regurgitation and atrial septal defect
D. Mitral arcade, isolated tricuspid stenosis, pulmonary vein stenosis and ductus

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Supravalvular mitral ring, parachute mitral valve, subaortic stenosis and coarctation

The classic four lesions are left-sided inflow/outflow obstructions; not all need be present or functionally significant.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

An infant has congenital mitral obstruction but no opening snap or loud first sound. Why does that not exclude the lesion?

A. Absence of both sounds proves unobstructed left atrial emptying
B. An opening snap is necessary in every form of mitral obstruction
C. Congenital obstruction necessarily causes a louder S1 than rheumatic disease
D. The abnormal leaflet apparatus may not make the brisk opening and closing movements required for those sounds

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

The abnormal leaflet apparatus may not make the brisk opening and closing movements required for those sounds

The characteristic brisk motion of a mobile anterior leaflet is not a feature of the congenital lesion described.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

An intra-atrial membrane is proximal to the left atrial appendage and mitral valve. Which lesion is favored over a supravalvular mitral ring?

A. A parachute valve defined by one papillary muscle
B. Cor triatriatum
C. Isolated pulmonary vein stenosis without an atrial membrane
D. A ring at the bases of the mitral leaflets

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Cor triatriatum

A membrane proximal to the appendage and valve distinguishes typical cor triatriatum from a supravalvular ring.

## Authored MCQ

A child has pulmonary hypertension, uneven pulmonary venous congestion and a normal-sized left atrium. Which mechanism best explains the combination?

A. Unequal stenosis of individual pulmonary veins proximal to the left atrium
B. An exclusively systemic venous obstruction below the diaphragm
C. Uniform mitral obstruction producing the same pressure upstream in every lung region
D. A large left atrial volume-overload lesion as the required cause

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Unequal stenosis of individual pulmonary veins proximal to the left atrium

Individual veins can have different severities of narrowing, producing regional congestion while the downstream left atrium remains normal-sized.

</details>
