## Authored MCQ

Imaging shows aortic atresia and a very small left ventricle. Which route supports systemic output before palliation?

A. Coronary sinus directly into the systemic arterial tree
B. Pulmonary veins directly into the descending aorta
C. Right ventricle through pulmonary trunk, ductus and descending aorta
D. Left ventricle through a normally patent aortic valve

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Right ventricle through pulmonary trunk, ductus and descending aorta

The pulmonary artery/ductus/descending aortic continuity provides systemic flow; retrograde flow supplies the small ascending aorta and coronary origins.

## Authored MCQ

A newborn with aortic atresia deteriorates as the ductus becomes restrictive. What linked mechanism is most concerning?

A. A necessary reduction of all pulmonary venous pressure with restored systemic flow
B. Spontaneous establishment of a normal two-ventricle circuit
C. Reduced systemic and retrograde coronary perfusion impairs output and RV function
D. Improved flow through the atretic aortic valve

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Reduced systemic and retrograde coronary perfusion impairs output and RV function

The ductus supports systemic and coronary supply; constriction compromises both and can worsen the sole pumping ventricle.

## Authored MCQ

An infant has an adequate atrial communication and patent ductus. Pulmonary flow rises and saturation improves, but systemic perfusion deteriorates. Which explanation fits?

A. Pulmonary flow has no effect on systemic flow distribution
B. The improved saturation establishes that the left ventricle has recovered
C. Flow is preferentially diverted to the lungs at the expense of systemic output
D. Higher saturation proves normal systemic oxygen delivery

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Flow is preferentially diverted to the lungs at the expense of systemic output

The right ventricle supplies both beds; increasing lung flow can improve mixing saturation while reducing systemic blood flow.

The source emphasizes unimpressive auscultation despite dramatic illness; aortic atresia leaves a single pulmonary closure sound.

## Authored MCQ

A newborn has severe mitral hypoplasia or atresia and a restrictive foramen ovale. Which consequence should be anticipated?

A. All pulmonary venous blood enters the systemic ventricle through a normal mitral valve
B. Pulmonary venous return encounters an obstructed left atrium
C. An intact atrial septum guarantees decompression
D. The ductus directly drains the left atrium

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Pulmonary venous return encounters an obstructed left atrium

The left atrium needs an alternative exit when mitral inflow is severely limited; atrial restriction compromises that exit and promotes congestion.
