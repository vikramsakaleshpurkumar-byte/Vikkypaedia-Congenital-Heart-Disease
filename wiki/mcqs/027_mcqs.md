## Authored MCQ

An adult has cyanosis and a pulmonary artery-to-vein communication but normal chamber size and near-normal cardiac output. Which explanation fits?

A. Every fistula necessarily doubles total cardiac output
B. The lesion requires a large intracardiac septal defect
C. Blood bypasses gas exchange while flow through uninvolved lung may fall reciprocally
D. The draining vein carries only fully oxygenated blood despite the bypass

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Blood bypasses gas exchange while flow through uninvolved lung may fall reciprocally

The right-to-left intrapulmonary route bypasses the capillary bed; redistribution can limit the net increase in ventricular flow.

## Authored MCQ

A cyanotic patient has a faint systolic-to-continuous sound over a lower lateral chest region that increases with inspiration. What interpretation is appropriate?

A. No murmur outside the precordium can have vascular significance
B. A peripheral pulmonary fistula can generate a localized flow murmur
C. Every continuous sound must arise from a ductus at the left upper sternal border
D. An inspiratory increase proves an intracardiac valve lesion

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

A peripheral pulmonary fistula can generate a localized flow murmur

Peripheral fistulae transmit sounds to the overlying chest wall; inspiratory flow and reduced compression may increase audibility.

Agitated saline enters the right heart and appears in the left atrium only after more than three cardiac cycles. What localization is suggested?

A. An immediate large atrial septal right-to-left jet
B. A normal capillary filter with no shunt as the only explanation
C. An isolated left-to-right ventricular shunt
D. A pulmonary right-to-left shunt

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

A pulmonary right-to-left shunt

The chapter describes delayed appearance beyond three cycles as usually indicating pulmonary shunting; the timing is a clue interpreted with anatomy.
