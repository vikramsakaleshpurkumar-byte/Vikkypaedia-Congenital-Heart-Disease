## Authored MCQ

A large nonrestrictive interatrial communication equalizes atrial pressure. Which factor principally directs the resulting shunt?

A. Relative compliance of the two ventricles
B. The side of the aortic arch as the principal determinant
C. Defect diameter alone after it is nonrestrictive
D. A large persistent interatrial pressure gradient as a prerequisite

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Relative compliance of the two ventricles

With little atrial pressure difference, preferential filling of the more compliant right ventricle produces the usual left-to-right shunt.

## Authored MCQ

An uncomplicated nonrestrictive atrial defect causes a superficial upper-left-sternal midsystolic murmur. What is its principal origin?

A. Aortic regurgitation in diastole
B. High-velocity flow through the nonrestrictive atrial opening itself
C. Left atrioventricular regurgitation as an obligatory mechanism
D. Increased flow through the pulmonary outflow and pulmonary trunk

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Increased flow through the pulmonary outflow and pulmonary trunk

The systolic murmur reflects augmented pulmonary flow, not a high-velocity jet across the nonrestrictive atrial communication.

PDF p. 210
</details>

## Authored MCQ

S2 is widely split in expiration and the separation stays unchanged through active inspiration. Which description is accurate?

A. Normal inspiratory widening only
B. Wide fixed splitting
C. A single second sound
D. Persistent splitting that widens with inspiration

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Wide fixed splitting

Fixed denotes unchanged separation with respiration; persistent splitting remains audible but varies in width.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A severely cyanotic dyspneic neonate has dramatic diffuse pulmonary venous congestion but little cardiac enlargement and a quiet pulmonary flow murmur. Which lesion is most concerning?

A. An uncomplicated large secundum defect as the sole explanation
B. Isolated mild pulmonary valve stenosis
C. Nonobstructed anomalous return with the typical later snowman silhouette
D. Obstructed total anomalous pulmonary venous connection

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Obstructed total anomalous pulmonary venous connection

Obstructed anomalous venous return can produce severe neonatal hypoxemia and congestion with an unimpressive heart size and attenuated flow murmur.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

Bridging leaflets attach to neither the atrial nor ventricular septum. What shunt arrangement follows?

A. An atrial-level communication only from leaflet attachment to the ventricular crest
B. No septal communication because the leaflets are free floating
C. A ventricular-level communication only from leaflet attachment to the atrial septum
D. Communication at both atrial and ventricular levels

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Communication at both atrial and ventricular levels

Free-floating bridging leaflets permit both atrial and ventricular components of the complete canal.

</details>
