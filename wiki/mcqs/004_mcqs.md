A fetus develops isolated complete atrioventricular block in a pregnancy with maternal anti-Ro/SSA antibodies. Which mechanism best matches the source?

A. Transplacental transfer of maternal IgG with injury to fetal conduction tissue
B. Direct transfer of maternal atrial electrical impulses into the fetus
C. A diagnosis that requires the mother already to have overt symptomatic lupus
D. Proof that the fetus must have a gross septal defect

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Transplacental transfer of maternal IgG with injury to fetal conduction tissue

The source describes passive autoimmunity involving maternal IgG antibodies, including Ro/SSA and La/SSB associations; maternal lupus can become apparent only later.

## Authored MCQ

A child with an inappropriately slow regular pulse has P waves occurring independently of the QRS complexes. Which interpretation best fits?

A. A fixed 2:1 atrioventricular relationship is established
B. Every atrial impulse conducts with a fixed long PR interval
C. The tracing proves physiologic sinus bradycardia with intact conduction
D. Atrial and ventricular activation are independent

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Atrial and ventricular activation are independent

In complete heart block, P waves and QRS complexes occur independently, rather than maintaining a fixed conducted relationship.

## Authored MCQ

Intermittently, a large jugular A wave appears when atrial systole coincides with ventricular systole. Which mechanism explains it?

A. Aortic regurgitation directly entering the jugular vein
B. Continuous pulmonary artery flow throughout diastole
C. Right atrial contraction against a closed tricuspid valve
D. Proof of a normally synchronized atrioventricular sequence

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Right atrial contraction against a closed tricuspid valve

Independent atrial and ventricular activity can bring atrial contraction against the closed tricuspid valve, abruptly amplifying the venous A wave.

## Authored MCQ

A child has a slow regular ventricular rhythm but markedly variable first-sound intensity. Which explanation is most appropriate?

A. A pure heart-rate change is required before sound intensity can vary
B. The changing atrial–ventricular timing alters valve position at ventricular systole
C. A fixed PR relationship produces identical valve timing
D. The finding excludes atrioventricular dissociation

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

The changing atrial–ventricular timing alters valve position at ventricular systole

Because atria and ventricles beat independently, the PR relationship changes and first-sound intensity can vary widely.
