## Authored MCQ

An adult develops obstruction from a valve known to have been congenitally bicuspid but functionally normal in childhood. Which principle best explains this course?

A. A congenital lesion must cause identical findings at every age
B. Congenital anatomy can acquire changing physiologic expression over time
C. A congenital lesion cannot change after fetal development
D. Adult presentation excludes a congenital origin

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Congenital anatomy can acquire changing physiologic expression over time

A congenital malformation may evolve during gestation and extrauterine life; a bicuspid valve can become stenotic decades after birth.

A child has a congenital conduction disorder without a major structural malformation. Which interpretation is most appropriate?

A. Every congenital cardiac disorder must produce a murmur
B. Congenital functional abnormalities can exist without a gross structural defect
C. The disorder cannot be congenital if the chambers look normal
D. Normal gross anatomy proves normal conduction

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

The source includes congenital complete heart block among functionally significant conditions that need not be gross structural defects.

## Authored MCQ

A lesion tolerated in fetal life becomes clinically important after birth. Which normal transition helps explain the change in its physiology?

A. Lung expansion and falling pulmonary vascular resistance redistribute flow
B. Placental gas exchange normally becomes the main postnatal oxygen source
C. The fetal and postnatal circulations have identical flow relationships
D. Pulmonary vascular resistance normally rises as the lungs expand

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Lung expansion and falling pulmonary vascular resistance redistribute flow

Lung expansion, rising oxygen tension, reduced pulmonary resistance and elimination of the placental circulation substantially alter cardiovascular physiology.

## Authored MCQ

Detailed imaging becomes available during an assessment. Which approach best preserves sound clinical reasoning?

A. Integrate the imaging with prior clinical observations and predicted physiology
B. Treat each investigation as an unrelated result
C. Diagnose solely from the anatomic label without considering physiology
D. Discard discordant bedside findings without explanation

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Integrate the imaging with prior clinical observations and predicted physiology

The diagnostic approach synthesizes history, examination, ECG, radiography and advanced imaging into a coherent anatomic–physiologic explanation.

A. Examine while calm and integrate the history and other findings afterward

The depth and synthesis of analysis matter more than a rigid order; examining an infant during a calm interval may improve the assessment.
