A radiograph shows a focal convexity immediately below the pulmonary trunk while the rest of the heart is not displaced. Which source-described explanation fits?

A. Proof that both atrial appendages have right morphology
B. An isolated neck-vein flow vibration
C. Herniation of the left atrial appendage through a partial left pericardial defect
D. Obligatory complete rightward displacement of the entire heart

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Herniation of the left atrial appendage through a partial left pericardial defect

Partial absence of the left pericardium may permit left atrial appendage herniation, producing the described convex left-border shadow.

## Authored MCQ

A mobile displaced heart has enlarged right-sided chambers without a demonstrated primary septal lesion. Which source-described physiologic explanation should be considered?

A. Reduced pericardial restraint particularly affects the relatively thin-walled right-sided chambers
B. Right-sided enlargement alone proves an atrial septal defect
C. The pericardium normally has no effect on filling or chamber dimensions
D. Leftward displacement by itself establishes a ventricular septal defect

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Reduced pericardial restraint particularly affects the relatively thin-walled right-sided chambers

Loss of normal pericardial constraint particularly affects the right ventricle and right atrium and can alter systemic venous return and septal motion.
