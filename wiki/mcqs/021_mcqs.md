## Authored MCQ

An infant has an imperforate pulmonary valve and no ventricular communication. Which route permits systemic venous blood to enter the systemic output?

A. Left-to-right flow through a ventricular defect that is absent
B. Aortic-to-left atrial flow as the required systemic venous route
C. Forward ejection through the sealed pulmonary valve
D. Right-to-left flow through an interatrial communication

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Right-to-left flow through an interatrial communication

The interatrial communication is the right atrium’s exit to the left heart; its restriction can add to the circulatory problem.

## Authored MCQ

A cyanotic newborn has no antegrade right ventricular-to-pulmonary flow and relies on an aorta-to-pulmonary ductal connection. What happens as that channel closes?

A. Normal pulmonary flow is guaranteed through an intact septum
B. Effective pulmonary blood flow can cease
C. The right ventricle automatically creates a new outlet
D. Collateral flow is always sufficient for survival

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Effective pulmonary blood flow can cease

The ductus supplies the pulmonary circuit in the usual anatomy; systemic-to-pulmonary collaterals are generally inadequate.

Two infants have the same sealed pulmonary outlet. One has a small thick-walled right ventricle; the other has a large thin-walled right ventricle with severe tricuspid regurgitation. Which interpretation is correct?

A. Both patterns occur, and tricuspid competence helps explain their different appearances
B. The large-ventricle pattern excludes the diagnosis
C. A small cavity proves the tricuspid annulus must be normal-sized
D. Every affected infant has identical tripartite anatomy

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Both patterns occur, and tricuspid competence helps explain their different appearances

The chapter describes a common small-ventricle pattern and a less common dilated pattern associated with an incompetent dysplastic or Ebstein-like valve.

## Authored MCQ

Coronary ostial discontinuity and severe proximal narrowing coexist with ventriculocoronary connections. Which perfusion mechanism becomes essential?

A. A low right ventricular pressure always improves the dependent perfusion
B. The connections can only be harmless incidental channels
C. Normal aortic coronary perfusion is necessarily intact despite ostial discontinuity
D. High right ventricular systolic pressure drives retrograde coronary flow

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

High right ventricular systolic pressure drives retrograde coronary flow

When aortic access is obstructed, myocardial perfusion can depend on suprasystemic right ventricular pressure supplying the coronary bed.
