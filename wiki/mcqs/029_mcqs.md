Imaging finds a coronary artery arising from an unexpected aortic sinus. What additional distinction is most important before assigning its clinical significance?

A. Whether its course is anterior, retroaortic, interarterial or intramyocardial
B. Only whether the ostium is on the right or left
C. Only the patient’s age without defining anatomy
D. Assume every ectopic origin has identical risk

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Whether its course is anterior, retroaortic, interarterial or intramyocardial

The source emphasizes that the route relative to the great arteries can be more important than the ectopic origin itself.

## Authored MCQ

A young adult has exertional symptoms. Which reported anatomic arrangement is most concerning in the source’s comparison?

A. Separate LAD and circumflex ostia in the left sinus with normal distribution
B. Left main from the right sinus passing between aorta and RV outflow tract
C. A small conus branch arising independently from the right sinus
D. A circumflex artery taking a retroaortic course from the right sinus

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Left main from the right sinus passing between aorta and RV outflow tract

The source identifies the left main or major left branch passing between the aorta and RV outflow tract as particularly concerning; ostial shape and exercise also affect risk.

## Authored MCQ

The LAD and circumflex arise through separate patent ostia in the left aortic sinus and follow normal distributions. Which interpretation fits?

A. A single coronary ostium supplying the entire heart
B. Left main atresia requiring all flow to traverse collaterals
C. Absence of a common left main trunk, generally functionally benign
D. An obligatory pulmonary arterial origin of the left system

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Absence of a common left main trunk, generally functionally benign

Separate patent LAD and circumflex origins are different from a blind or imperforate left main origin.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A patient with tetralogy has an anomalous coronary branch crossing the planned RV outflow operative field, without separate ischemic symptoms. Why must it be mapped?

A. It may create major surgical risk despite little baseline physiologic effect
B. Absence of ischemic symptoms makes its route irrelevant
C. An ECG alone identifies its exact course for the surgeon
D. Every such anomaly causes the entire cyanosis mechanism

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

It may create major surgical risk despite little baseline physiologic effect

A coronary crossing the outflow tract can be endangered during intervention even when its resting physiologic effect is small.

## Authored MCQ

An infant has an imperforate left main origin and a nonhypoplastic distal left coronary bed. Which connection is essential to perfuse that bed?

A. An intact atrial septum as the direct arterial supply
B. An absent coronary venous system alone
C. A pulmonary capillary network directly replacing all coronary arteries
D. Adequate channels from a normally arising right coronary artery

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Adequate channels from a normally arising right coronary artery

Survival depends on adequate connections from the right coronary artery to the distal left system; presentation can span infancy to adulthood.

</details>
