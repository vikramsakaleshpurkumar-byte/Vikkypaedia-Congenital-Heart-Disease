## Authored MCQ

Imaging shows right atrium to morphologic left ventricle to pulmonary trunk, and left atrium to morphologic right ventricle to aorta. Which connection pattern explains this?

A. Atrioventricular discordance with ventriculoarterial concordance
B. Concordance at both junctions
C. Atrioventricular concordance with ventriculoarterial discordance
D. Atrioventricular and ventriculoarterial discordance

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Atrioventricular and ventriculoarterial discordance

Two discordant junctions place systemic venous blood in the pulmonary circuit and pulmonary venous blood in the systemic circuit, so the circulations are in series.

## Authored MCQ

An adult has a subaortic morphologic right ventricle. Why can normal oxygenation coexist with later ventricular dysfunction?

A. The morphologic left ventricle necessarily remains the systemic pump
B. The atrioventricular discordance removes the workload imposed by systemic arterial resistance
C. The systemic morphologic right ventricle must sustain a workload for which its usual geometry and blood supply are not designed
D. The subaortic right ventricle has the same geometry and coronary supply-demand relation as a normal systemic left ventricle

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

The systemic morphologic right ventricle must sustain a workload for which its usual geometry and blood supply are not designed

The systemic right ventricle retains right-ventricular geometry and a morphologically right coronary supply despite systemic loading.

## Authored MCQ

A patient with ventricular inversion develops regurgitation across an Ebstein-like left atrioventricular valve. Which valve is involved?

A. Morphologic mitral valve
B. Aortic valve
C. Pulmonary valve
D. Morphologic tricuspid valve

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Morphologic tricuspid valve

The inverted left atrioventricular valve is morphologically tricuspid; Ebstein-like abnormalities and age-related regurgitation are important.

## Authored MCQ

A child with an anomalous anterior atrioventricular node develops increasing conduction delay during adolescence. Which substrate is described?

A. An isolated accessory pathway as the only possible conduction abnormality
B. Displacement of the sinoatrial node as the necessary cause
C. Replacement of conduction fibers by fibrous tissue in the long penetrating bundle
D. Physiologic prolongation of conduction that cannot progress to higher-grade block

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Replacement of conduction fibers by fibrous tissue in the long penetrating bundle

Fibrous replacement in the long penetrating bundle contributes to age-related atrioventricular block; defects may occur at several conduction levels.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

An ECG suggests ventricular inversion through reversal of normal septal activation. Which Q-wave distribution supports that mechanism?

A. Identical septal Q-wave distribution to the normal heart
B. Absent right precordial Q waves with normal left precordial Q waves
C. Q waves in right precordial leads with absent left precordial Q waves
D. Q waves confined to lateral leads as the defining reversal

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Q waves in right precordial leads with absent left precordial Q waves

Inverted bundle branches activate the septum from right to left, reversing the usual precordial septal Q-wave pattern.

PDF p. 78
</details>
