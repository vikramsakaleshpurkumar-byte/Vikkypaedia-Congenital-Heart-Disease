## Authored MCQ

An interventricular communication permits left-to-right flow, but left ventricular systolic pressure remains higher than right ventricular pressure. Which description applies?

A. Nonrestrictive defect with equal ventricular pressures
B. Reversed shunt established solely by the pressure difference
C. Restrictive defect
D. An obligatory atrial-level shunt

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Restrictive defect

Restriction at the defect limits pressure transmission and shunt flow, leaving a systolic pressure difference.

## Authored MCQ

An infant was well at birth but now sweats and tires during feeds, with poor growth and a large ventricular communication. Which transition explains the timing?

A. A normal neonatal examination proves the defect was acquired later
B. Falling pulmonary vascular resistance increases left-to-right flow
C. The left ventricle becomes protected from all volume loading after birth
D. A physiologic rise in pulmonary resistance invariably increases pulmonary flow

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Falling pulmonary vascular resistance increases left-to-right flow

The neonatal fall in pulmonary resistance permits a larger shunt and pulmonary venous return, imposing left ventricular volume load.

## Authored MCQ

An adult with an unrepaired large ventricular communication develops cyanosis as pulmonary vascular resistance becomes suprasystemic. What mechanism fits?

A. Increased left-to-right flow as the sole cause of systemic desaturation
B. An isolated fall in systemic oxygen demand
C. Spontaneous complete closure producing the cyanosis
D. Right-to-left shunting from pulmonary vascular disease

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Right-to-left shunting from pulmonary vascular disease

With equal ventricular systolic pressures, relative vascular resistances govern flow; suprasystemic pulmonary resistance favors reversal.

## Authored MCQ

A patient with a large ventricular communication develops a shorter, softer murmur and more cyanosis. Which interpretation is best?

A. Softening alone proves spontaneous closure
B. Increasing cyanosis proves the shunt is now larger left-to-right
C. A quieter murmur guarantees lower pulmonary pressure
D. Reduced left-to-right flow can reflect worsening pulmonary vascular disease

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Reduced left-to-right flow can reflect worsening pulmonary vascular disease

As pulmonary resistance approaches systemic, the left-to-right murmur can shorten, soften and eventually disappear with reversal.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A child with a known small ventricular communication develops an early diastolic murmur and left ventricular enlargement out of proportion to the shunt. What should echocardiography assess?

A. Complete closure as proof that no valve lesion can exist
B. Aortic cusp prolapse and regurgitation
C. Isolated pulmonary valve stenosis as the only explanation
D. Mitral stenosis as an obligatory complication

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Aortic cusp prolapse and regurgitation

Perimembranous and subarterial defects can be associated with aortic cusp prolapse; regurgitant volume can explain disproportionate left ventricular enlargement.

</details>
