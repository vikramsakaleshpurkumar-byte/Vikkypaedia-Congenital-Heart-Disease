## Authored MCQ

A left coronary artery arises from the pulmonary trunk. Why might myocardial perfusion worsen after an initially well neonatal period?

A. Falling pulmonary pressure reduces antegrade perfusion through the anomalous artery
B. A single normal aortic coronary origin alone invariably causes ischemia
C. A fall in pulmonary pressure necessarily increases its perfusion pressure
D. Low oxygen content alone explains the entire mechanism regardless of flow

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Falling pulmonary pressure reduces antegrade perfusion through the anomalous artery

Initially high pulmonary pressure can support antegrade flow. Its postnatal fall reduces that supply unless intercoronary perfusion becomes adequate.

## Authored MCQ

A normal aortic right coronary supplies an anomalous left coronary through collaterals, and blood then runs into the pulmonary trunk. Which interpretation is best?

A. The left coronary must still receive only antegrade pulmonary blood
B. Collaterals restore some perfusion but also permit a low-resistance coronary steal
C. Collateral presence guarantees completely normal myocardial perfusion
D. Runoff into the pulmonary artery is impossible once collaterals develop

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Collaterals restore some perfusion but also permit a low-resistance coronary steal

The collateral route can nourish myocardium while also bypassing capillaries toward a low-pressure pulmonary outlet.

## Authored MCQ

An infant has left ventricular dysfunction and an apical holosystolic murmur. An anomalous coronary origin is suspected. Which mechanism links the valve finding?

A. Ischemic papillary muscle dysfunction
B. Pulmonary valve regurgitation transmitted unchanged to the apex
C. Aortic runoff directly through a ventricular septal defect
D. Obligatory congenital mitral commissural fusion

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Ischemic papillary muscle dysfunction

Papillary muscle ischemia can impair mitral competence and add regurgitant volume to an already compromised ventricle.

## Authored MCQ

A young infant with feeding-related pallor and ventricular dysfunction has deep narrow q waves in leads I and aVL. Which diagnosis deserves focused assessment?

A. Isolated pulmonary valve stenosis as the typical cause of lateral infarct-like q waves
B. Anomalous left coronary artery from the pulmonary trunk
C. An innocent flow murmur with normal myocardial perfusion
D. An uncomplicated atrial-level shunt as the sufficient explanation

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Anomalous left coronary artery from the pulmonary trunk

Deep narrow lateral q waves, often with left ventricular hypertrophy and a leftward axis, are a characteristic clue; early infant tracings can be less conspicuous.

## Authored MCQ

Diastolic flow enters the pulmonary trunk near the pulmonary valve and the right coronary is dilated. Which additional observation most directly clarifies the diagnosis?

A. Use mitral regurgitation alone to establish the coronary origin
B. Use left ventricular size alone to locate both ostia
C. Assume every pulmonary arterial diastolic jet is a ductus
D. Define whether the left coronary arises from the pulmonary trunk or from its normal aortic sinus

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Define whether the left coronary arises from the pulmonary trunk or from its normal aortic sinus

An anomalous left coronary lacks its normal aortic origin; a coronary arterial fistula can have similar pulmonary flow while both coronary arteries retain aortic origins.
