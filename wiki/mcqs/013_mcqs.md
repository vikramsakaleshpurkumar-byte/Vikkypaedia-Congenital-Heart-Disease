## Authored MCQ

A patient with severe pulmonary valve stenosis has central cyanosis from an atrial-level right-to-left shunt. Which interatrial pathway is most typical in the source?

A. An intact atrial septum
B. Patent foramen ovale
C. An isolated coronary sinus unroofing as the usual required pathway
D. Nonrestrictive secundum defect with the usual large left-to-right shunt

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Patent foramen ovale

Severe pulmonary stenosis with right-to-left shunting most often uses a patent foramen ovale; a nonrestrictive secundum defect more often accompanies mild-to-moderate stenosis and left-to-right flow.

## Authored MCQ

A hypertrophied pressure-loaded right ventricle is filled by vigorous right atrial contraction. Which mechanism can produce presystolic atrial-level escape flow?

A. Pulmonary venous return bypasses both atria directly
B. Atrial contraction necessarily closes every PFO permanently
C. High right atrial pressure stretches the foramen and drives blood toward the left atrium
D. A fall in right atrial pressure drives blood from right to left

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

High right atrial pressure stretches the foramen and drives blood toward the left atrium

Forceful atrial contraction raises right atrial pressure, increases PFO patency and can generate a presystolic right-to-left shunt.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A patient with severe pulmonary stenosis has cold bluish hands and feet. After warming, that peripheral discoloration diminishes. Which interpretation fits?

A. Peripheral cyanosis must worsen as skin flow improves
B. The response proves absence of any right-to-left shunt
C. Warming has necessarily closed the atrial communication
D. A low-skin-flow peripheral component has improved; central shunt cyanosis must still be assessed

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

A low-skin-flow peripheral component has improved; central shunt cyanosis must still be assessed

Warmth improves skin blood flow and can diminish peripheral cyanosis while making any persistent central component easier to recognize.

## Authored MCQ

A patient has a nonrestrictive atrial defect, but the pulmonary systolic murmur is longer and louder than expected, with an ejection sound and a more delayed softer P2. What should be sought?

A. An isolated restrictive mitral ring as the source of the pulmonary ejection sound
B. Only increased flow through a structurally normal valve as a complete explanation
C. Aortic regurgitation as the cause of the delayed pulmonary closure
D. Coexisting mobile pulmonary valve stenosis

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Coexisting mobile pulmonary valve stenosis

The combination adds valvar ejection sound, a longer louder stenotic murmur and further delay and softening of P2 to the atrial-shunt findings.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

Pulmonary valve stenosis and a PFO are seen on two-dimensional imaging. Which complementary assessment is appropriate?

A. Doppler for the obstructive gradient and color flow for the interatrial shunt
B. Use the systolic murmur grade as a substitute for defining flow direction
C. Use atrial opening size alone as a complete measurement of valve obstruction
D. Infer both severity and shunt direction from cyanosis intensity alone

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Doppler for the obstructive gradient and color flow for the interatrial shunt

Imaging identifies the valve and interatrial pathway; Doppler assesses the gradient and color flow confirms shunt direction.

</details>
