<details><summary>How is the systemic circulation supplied in the aortic-atresia form of hypoplastic left heart?</summary>

Right ventricle through pulmonary trunk, ductus and descending aorta. The pulmonary artery/ductus/descending aortic continuity provides systemic flow; retrograde flow supplies the small ascending aorta and coronary origins.

</details>

<details><summary>Why is ductal constriction dangerous in aortic-atresia hypoplastic left heart?</summary>

Reduced systemic and retrograde coronary perfusion impairs output and RV function. The ductus supports systemic and coronary supply; constriction compromises both and can worsen the sole pumping ventricle.

PDF p. 503
</details>

<details><summary>Why can falling pulmonary resistance produce a shock-like state despite relatively high oxygen saturation?</summary>

Flow is preferentially diverted to the lungs at the expense of systemic output. The right ventricle supplies both beds; increasing lung flow can improve mixing saturation while reducing systemic blood flow.

PDF p. 505
</details>

<details><summary>Can hypoplastic left heart present with little or no murmur?</summary>

A severe ductal-dependent left-heart lesion remains possible. The source emphasizes unimpressive auscultation despite dramatic illness; aortic atresia leaves a single pulmonary closure sound.

PDF p. 506
</details>

<details><summary>Why does restrictive left atrial egress matter in hypoplastic left heart?</summary>

Pulmonary venous return encounters an obstructed left atrium. The left atrium needs an alternative exit when mitral inflow is severely limited; atrial restriction compromises that exit and promotes congestion.

PDF p. 503
</details>

<details><summary>Which echocardiographic findings should be assessed beyond left ventricular size?</summary>

Define the aortic and mitral valves, atrial septum, ductal and arch pathways, retrograde ascending aortic flow, RV function and tricuspid competence.
