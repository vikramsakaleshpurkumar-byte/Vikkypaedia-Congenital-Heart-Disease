<details><summary>Which atrioventricular and ventriculoarterial connections define complete transposition?</summary>

AV concordance with ventriculoarterial discordance and parallel circuits. Complete transposition combines AV concordance and ventriculoarterial discordance, creating parallel systemic and pulmonary recirculation.

PDF p. 451
</details>

<details><summary>Why can increasing pulmonary flow fail to correct systemic hypoxemia when the atrial communication is restrictive?</summary>

Oxygenated blood recirculates through the pulmonary circuit instead of adequately entering systemic flow. An adequate communication permits exchange between the circuits; a restrictive atrial route can leave oxygenated blood trapped in pulmonary recirculation.

PDF p. 457
</details>

<details><summary>Can an early neonatal ECG be normal in complete transposition?</summary>

A normal early ECG does not exclude the abnormal arterial connections. The chapter explicitly describes a potentially normal ECG in the first days; structural assessment remains essential.

PDF p. 461
</details>

<details><summary>What can cause reversed differential cyanosis in complete transposition with a large ductus and pulmonary vascular disease?</summary>

More oxygenated pulmonary arterial blood enters the descending aorta beyond the left subclavian artery. Oxygenated pulmonary arterial blood can pass through the ductus to the lower body, producing less cyanotic feet.

PDF p. 458
</details>

<details><summary>What produces the characteristic egg-like cardiac silhouette in complete transposition with increased pulmonary flow?</summary>

A prominent right atrium and convex enlarged left ventricle shape the silhouette. The right atrium forms the blunter right border and the enlarged left ventricle the convex left border; arterial arrangement contributes to the narrow pedicle.

PDF p. 464
</details>

<details><summary>Why is coronary anatomy important even if it does not determine the initial physiology of complete transposition?</summary>

The origins and proximal courses are anatomically pivotal for an arterial switch operation; they must be defined rather than assumed from the usual pattern.

</details>
