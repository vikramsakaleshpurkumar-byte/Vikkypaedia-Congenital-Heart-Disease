<details><summary>Why does an ectopic coronary origin require definition of the proximal course?</summary>

Whether its course is anterior, retroaortic, interarterial or intramyocardial. The source emphasizes that the route relative to the great arteries can be more important than the ectopic origin itself.

<details><summary>Which anomalous coronary arrangement carries the greatest risk in the chapter’s comparison?</summary>

Left main from the right sinus passing between aorta and RV outflow tract. The source identifies the left main or major left branch passing between the aorta and RV outflow tract as particularly concerning; ostial shape and exercise also affect risk.

</details>

<details><summary>How does absence of a left main trunk differ from left main ostial atresia?</summary>

Absence of a common left main trunk, generally functionally benign. Separate patent LAD and circumflex origins are different from a blind or imperforate left main origin.

PDF p. 515
</details>

<details><summary>Why is a coronary artery crossing the RV outflow tract important in tetralogy of Fallot?</summary>

It may create major surgical risk despite little baseline physiologic effect. A coronary crossing the outflow tract can be endangered during intervention even when its resting physiologic effect is small.

PDF p. 519
</details>

<details><summary>What permits survival when the left main coronary ostium is congenitally atretic?</summary>

Adequate channels from a normally arising right coronary artery. Survival depends on adequate connections from the right coronary artery to the distal left system; presentation can span infancy to adulthood.

PDF p. 518
</details>

<details><summary>Why is a single coronary artery not automatically equivalent to clinically dangerous coronary flow?</summary>

A single ostium can give rise to normally formed and distributed branches; significance depends on factors including major branch hypoplasia and a potentially hazardous proximal course.

</details>
