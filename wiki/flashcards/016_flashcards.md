<details><summary>Where does left ventricular blood preferentially stream with a subpulmonary ventricular communication?</summary>

Left ventricular blood preferentially enters the pulmonary trunk. A subpulmonary communication directs left ventricular output toward the pulmonary outlet, creating a transposition-like physiology.

PDF p. 331
</details>

<details><summary>What physiology can subaortic double outlet right ventricle without pulmonary stenosis resemble?</summary>

A large ventricular left-to-right shunt with increased pulmonary flow. Low pulmonary resistance permits increased pulmonary flow and left ventricular volume overload, resembling a large isolated ventricular defect.

PDF p. 327
</details>

<details><summary>How does pulmonary stenosis change subaortic double outlet physiology?</summary>

Less pulmonary flow and more blood directed to the aorta. Obstruction diverts both ventricular streams away from the pulmonary outlet and toward the aorta, producing a tetralogy-like pattern.

PDF p. 327
</details>

<details><summary>Why can a restrictive ventricular communication be hazardous in double outlet right ventricle?</summary>

Obstruction to left ventricular egress and subaortic flow. The communication is an essential left ventricular exit; restriction can act as subaortic stenosis rather than a uniformly favorable reduction in shunt.

PDF p. 326
</details>

<details><summary>What does atrioventricular valve straddling mean?</summary>

Chordal attachments insert on both sides of the ventricular septum; this differs from overriding, which describes biventricular commitment of the valve annulus.
