Dysplastic pulmonary valve stenosis. Dysplastic pulmonary stenosis has thickened separate poorly mobile leaflets without commissural fusion.

<details><summary>How does a typical mobile pulmonary-valve ejection sound change with inspiration?</summary>

Reduced additional cephalad movement of a mobile pulmonary valve during ventricular contraction. Inspiration prepositions the mobile valve cephalad through increased right atrial contraction, reducing further excursion and the ejection sound.

PDF p. 161
</details>

<details><summary>Can pinpoint neonatal pulmonary stenosis produce a soft murmur?</summary>

Pinpoint pulmonary stenosis can have a deceptively soft murmur. The source emphasizes that pinpoint neonatal stenosis may sound disarmingly soft despite severe obstruction.

<details><summary>Why can the pulmonary component of S2 remain normal in branch pulmonary artery stenosis?</summary>

The central pulmonary diastolic pressure can remain normal or nearly normal at valve closure. Branch stenosis can raise central systolic pressure while pressure falls to near-normal diastolic levels, so valve closure need not be accentuated.

PDF p. 164
</details>

<details><summary>What diastolic murmur pattern is described in isolated low-pressure congenital pulmonary regurgitation?</summary>

Short delayed low-to-medium-frequency crescendo-decrescendo murmur at the upper left sternal border. The source describes a distinctive short delayed low-to-medium-frequency diastolic murmur, sometimes louder with inspiration.

</details>

<details><summary>How does a double-chambered right ventricle create obstruction?</summary>

Obstructing intracavitary muscle bundles divide the right ventricle into a high-pressure inlet portion and a lower-pressure outlet portion; a ventricular septal defect commonly coexists.
