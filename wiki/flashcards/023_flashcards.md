<details><summary>How does pulmonary flow influence presentation in a single-ventricle heart?</summary>

Excess pulmonary flow with ventricular volume overload. Excess pulmonary flow produces heart failure and poor growth despite relatively mild cyanosis.

Preferential streaming can direct oxygenated blood toward the aorta. The source describes partial stream separation influenced by outlet arrangement and pulmonary resistance.

<details><summary>Why can progressive restriction of the outlet foramen worsen single-left-ventricle physiology?</summary>

Subaortic obstruction with diversion toward the pulmonary circuit and greater volume burden. Restriction creates subaortic stenosis and may augment already excessive pulmonary flow and ventricular volume overload.

PDF p. 441
</details>

<details><summary>Why can a pulmonary stenotic murmur shorten and soften as obstruction worsens in a single-ventricle heart?</summary>

Less blood crosses the increasingly obstructed pulmonary outlet. As stenosis increases, more flow is diverted to the systemic outlet and less traverses the pulmonary obstruction, reducing its murmur.

PDF p. 441
</details>

<details><summary>What associated structure should be assessed when subaortic stenosis has existed from infancy in a single-left-ventricle heart?</summary>

The aortic arch should be assessed for coarctation or hypoplasia using appropriate imaging and Doppler, because these may accompany a restrictive systemic outlet.
