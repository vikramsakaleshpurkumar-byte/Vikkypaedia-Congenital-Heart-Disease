<details><summary>What mainly determines shunt volume across a nonrestrictive atrial septal defect?</summary>

Relative compliance of the two ventricles. With little atrial pressure difference, preferential filling of the more compliant right ventricle produces the usual left-to-right shunt.

PDF p. 204
</details>

<details><summary>Where does the typical midsystolic murmur of a nonrestrictive atrial septal defect originate?</summary>

Increased flow through the pulmonary outflow and pulmonary trunk. The systolic murmur reflects augmented pulmonary flow, not a high-velocity jet across the nonrestrictive atrial communication.

PDF p. 210
</details>

<details><summary>What does fixed splitting mean?</summary>

Wide fixed splitting. Fixed denotes unchanged separation with respiration; persistent splitting remains audible but varies in width.

PDF p. 211
</details>

<details><summary>What imaging-clinical combination supports obstructed total anomalous pulmonary venous connection?</summary>

Obstructed total anomalous pulmonary venous connection. Obstructed anomalous venous return can produce severe neonatal hypoxemia and congestion with an unimpressive heart size and attenuated flow murmur.

PDF p. 237
</details>

<details><summary>What leaflet arrangement permits both atrial and ventricular shunting in a complete common atrioventricular canal?</summary>

Communication at both atrial and ventricular levels. Free-floating bridging leaflets permit both atrial and ventricular components of the complete canal.

PDF p. 239
</details>

<details><summary>How does persistent PFO differ from a true secundum tissue defect?</summary>

A PFO retains a valve at the fossa ovalis that has not fused completely; a true secundum defect represents deficient septal tissue rather than simply persistence of the flap-valve channel.

PDF p. 197
</details>
