<details><summary>Why can ischemia emerge after the neonatal fall in pulmonary pressure?</summary>

Falling pulmonary pressure reduces antegrade perfusion through the anomalous artery. Initially high pulmonary pressure can support antegrade flow. Its postnatal fall reduces that supply unless intercoronary perfusion becomes adequate.

</details>

<details><summary>How can intercoronary collateral flow both help and harm in pulmonary-origin left coronary anatomy?</summary>

Collaterals restore some perfusion but also permit a low-resistance coronary steal. The collateral route can nourish myocardium while also bypassing capillaries toward a low-pressure pulmonary outlet.

PDF p. 374
</details>

<details><summary>Why can anomalous left coronary origin cause mitral regurgitation?</summary>

Ischemic papillary muscle dysfunction. Papillary muscle ischemia can impair mitral competence and add regurgitant volume to an already compromised ventricle.

PDF p. 377
</details>

<details><summary>Which ECG pattern should raise suspicion for pulmonary-origin left coronary artery in a symptomatic infant?</summary>

Anomalous left coronary artery from the pulmonary trunk. Deep narrow lateral q waves, often with left ventricular hypertrophy and a leftward axis, are a characteristic clue; early infant tracings can be less conspicuous.

PDF p. 379
</details>

<details><summary>What imaging observation separates anomalous coronary origin from a coronary-to-pulmonary fistula?</summary>

Define whether the left coronary arises from the pulmonary trunk or from its normal aortic sinus. An anomalous left coronary lacks its normal aortic origin; a coronary arterial fistula can have similar pulmonary flow while both coronary arteries retain aortic origins.

PDF p. 381
</details>

<details><summary>Does asymptomatic survival to adulthood establish that a pulmonary-origin left coronary artery is benign?</summary>

No. Adult presentations can include mitral regurgitation, ischemia, ventricular arrhythmia, cardiac arrest or sudden death despite little earlier symptom burden.

</details>
