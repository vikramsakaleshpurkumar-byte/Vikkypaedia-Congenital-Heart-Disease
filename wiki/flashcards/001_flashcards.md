<details><summary>Why can a congenital cardiac malformation first become clinically apparent in adulthood?</summary>

Congenital anatomy can acquire changing physiologic expression over time. A congenital malformation may evolve during gestation and extrauterine life; a bicuspid valve can become stenotic decades after birth.

</details>

<details><summary>Why does a normal gross cardiac structure not exclude a congenital functional cardiac disorder?</summary>

Congenital functional abnormalities can exist without a gross structural defect. The source includes congenital complete heart block among functionally significant conditions that need not be gross structural defects.

PDF p. 15
</details>

<details><summary>How does the fall in pulmonary vascular resistance after birth affect the interpretation of congenital circulation?</summary>

Lung expansion and falling pulmonary vascular resistance redistribute flow. Lung expansion, rising oxygen tension, reduced pulmonary resistance and elimination of the placental circulation substantially alter cardiovascular physiology.

PDF p. 15
</details>

<details><summary>How should advanced imaging relate to the history, examination, ECG and chest radiograph?</summary>

Integrate the imaging with prior clinical observations and predicted physiology. The diagnostic approach synthesizes history, examination, ECG, radiography and advanced imaging into a coherent anatomic–physiologic explanation.

PDF p. 16
</details>

<details><summary>Why may examination reasonably precede completion of the history in an irritable infant?</summary>

Examine while calm and integrate the history and other findings afterward. The depth and synthesis of analysis matter more than a rigid order; examining an infant during a calm interval may improve the assessment.

PDF p. 16
</details>

<details><summary>What are the five cornerstone questions used to organize congenital cardiac recognition?</summary>

Ask about cyanosis, increased or decreased pulmonary arterial flow, the side from which the malformation originates, the dominant ventricle, and pulmonary hypertension. These organize reasoning rather than replace anatomic confirmation.

</details>
