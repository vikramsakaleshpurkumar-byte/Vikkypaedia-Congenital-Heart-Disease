<details><summary>What typical combination accompanies severe pulmonary valve stenosis and a right-to-left atrial shunt?</summary>

Patent foramen ovale. Severe pulmonary stenosis with right-to-left shunting most often uses a patent foramen ovale; a nonrestrictive secundum defect more often accompanies mild-to-moderate stenosis and left-to-right flow.

</details>

<details><summary>How can severe pulmonary stenosis generate a presystolic right-to-left atrial shunt?</summary>

High right atrial pressure stretches the foramen and drives blood toward the left atrium. Forceful atrial contraction raises right atrial pressure, increases PFO patency and can generate a presystolic right-to-left shunt.

PDF p. 254
</details>

<details><summary>How does warmth help distinguish peripheral low-flow cyanosis from a central shunt contribution?</summary>

A low-skin-flow peripheral component has improved; central shunt cyanosis must still be assessed. Warmth improves skin blood flow and can diminish peripheral cyanosis while making any persistent central component easier to recognize.

</details>

<details><summary>What auscultatory additions suggest pulmonary valve stenosis in a nonrestrictive atrial septal defect?</summary>

Coexisting mobile pulmonary valve stenosis. The combination adds valvar ejection sound, a longer louder stenotic murmur and further delay and softening of P2 to the atrial-shunt findings.

</details>

<details><summary>Which imaging tools establish the valve obstruction and atrial shunt direction?</summary>

Doppler for the obstructive gradient and color flow for the interatrial shunt. Imaging identifies the valve and interatrial pathway; Doppler assesses the gradient and color flow confirms shunt direction.

</details>

<details><summary>Why does raised right atrial pressure with an atrial communication increase paradoxical embolic risk?</summary>

It can promote right-to-left passage across a PFO or atrial defect, allowing systemic venous embolic material access to the arterial circulation.
