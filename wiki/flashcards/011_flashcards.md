<details><summary>What is the atrialized right ventricle in Ebstein anatomy?</summary>

The inlet right ventricle between the anatomic annulus and displaced functional valve. Apical displacement atrializes the proximal inlet right ventricle while the trabecular and outlet portions form the functional right ventricle.

</details>

<details><summary>Why may prominent systolic jugular V waves be absent despite severe Ebstein-related regurgitation?</summary>

A large compliant right atrium and toneless atrialized ventricle damp low-pressure regurgitant flow. Commodious right-sided chambers and low-pressure hypokinetic regurgitation can damp the expected jugular signs.

PDF p. 183
</details>

<details><summary>How can neonatal cyanosis improve and recur years later in Ebstein anomaly?</summary>

A neonatal right-to-left atrial shunt can diminish as pulmonary resistance falls and reappear when abnormal right ventricular filling pressure rises. The source describes this uncommon course through changing right-sided filling conditions and interatrial shunting.

PDF p. 182
</details>

<details><summary>Which ECG finding supports preexcitation rather than short PR alone?</summary>

Preexcitation. Early delta-wave inscription supports preexcitation; a short PR interval alone can occasionally occur without a delta wave or tachycardia history.

PDF p. 187
</details>

<details><summary>What should echocardiography assess beyond displaced tricuspid leaflet anatomy?</summary>

Identify a PFO or secundum defect and determine interatrial shunt direction. Color flow imaging and Doppler identify the atrial pathway and whether right-to-left shunting coexists.

PDF p. 190
</details>

<details><summary>What distinctive electrical-mechanical combination characterizes the atrialized right ventricle?</summary>

It generates a ventricular intracavitary electrogram but an atrial-type pressure pulse, unlike the functional right ventricle.
