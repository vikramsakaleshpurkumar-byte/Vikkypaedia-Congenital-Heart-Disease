<details><summary>Why can a pulmonary arteriovenous fistula cause cyanosis without major ventricular volume overload?</summary>

Blood bypasses gas exchange while flow through uninvolved lung may fall reciprocally. The right-to-left intrapulmonary route bypasses the capillary bed; redistribution can limit the net increase in ventricular flow.

<details><summary>Why should auscultation extend beyond the precordium when a pulmonary AV fistula is suspected?</summary>

A peripheral pulmonary fistula can generate a localized flow murmur. Peripheral fistulae transmit sounds to the overlying chest wall; inspiratory flow and reduced compression may increase audibility.

<details><summary>What important neurologic risks accompany pulmonary AV fistulae?</summary>

Paradoxical embolism, stroke and brain abscess. The shunt bypasses the pulmonary filter; the source highlights embolic events and brain abscess.

<details><summary>What radiographic finding helps identify a pulmonary fistula rather than a nonspecific lung nodule?</summary>

A round or lobulated intrapulmonary density may have vascular shadows linking it to the hilum, representing afferent and efferent channels; small diffuse lesions may be poorly defined.
